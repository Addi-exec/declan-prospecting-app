Just sold street

You've just sold a property on their street. That result is your reason to call every neighbour. Full call flow from opener to close, plus follow-up for every scenario.

The 7-second framework — what research says
0–1s — Use their name correctly
1–3s — Gently acknowledge it's out of the blue
3–5s — Give them a reason to listen
5–7s — Connect to their specific world
Source: Gong analysis of 300M+ calls · Cold Call Genius framework · Cialdini's Pre-Suasion principles
1 — The warm reach-out
🔬 11.18% success rateBest all-round opener
"Hi [Name], it's Declan here — I'm one of the local agents around [suburb]. We haven't met, so I'll keep this genuine and quick. I just helped some of your neighbours sell their place on [street], and I'm personally letting a few people nearby know how it went before word gets around. Is now an okay moment for a quick one?"
Why it works: Naming that you haven't met — warmly, not apologetically — builds instant honesty and likeability (Cialdini's liking principle) without the defensiveness of announcing "a cold call." Framing it as letting them know "before word gets around" makes the call feel like a favour, and the soft "is now okay?" earns a small yes (micro-commitment) rather than forcing a pitch. A tracked study found ~30% of prospects stayed past 30 seconds with a genuine, permission-led opener vs 14% for a standard pitch.
Cialdini — liking (genuine honesty)Favour framingMicro-commitment ask
2 — The curiosity gap
🧠 Open loop psychologyBest for engaged streets
"Hi [Name], it's Declan — I'm a local agent here in [suburb]. I won't keep you long, but something just happened on your street that I reckon you'd genuinely want to know about. Have you got thirty seconds for me to explain?"
Why it works: Telling them there's something worth knowing — without saying what yet — creates an "open loop" the brain is wired to want closed, so they stay on to hear it. Keeping it warm and specific to "your street" makes the curiosity feel personal rather than like a gimmick.
If they say yes:
"So [address] just sold — and the result was well above what most people in the street would have guessed. Based on that, your property is probably sitting at [range]. I can give you a more accurate number in about 20 minutes if you ever wanted to know."
Open loop / curiosity gapWarm + personalInformation gap theory
3 — Permission + context
🔬 11.24% — #1 by data
"Hi [Name], Declan here — I know you weren't expecting my call. I sold [address] just recently and I'm reaching out to a few of the neighbours directly. If it's not relevant, just say the word and I'll leave you to it. Worth 20 seconds?"
Why it works: The permission ask creates a micro-commitment — once someone says "sure", they've psychologically opted in (self-determination theory). Offering a clean exit lowers resistance.
Self-determination theoryMicro-commitmentLow exit friction
4 — Reciprocity + social proof
Best for warm streets
"Hi [Name], Declan here. I just sold [address] — went in [X days, $X above reserve] — and a couple of neighbours flagged me down in the street and asked what it meant for their place. I figured rather than let rumour do the work, I'd just call a few people directly and share the data. Takes two minutes and I'm not here to sell you anything today."
Why it works: Leading with free data triggers Cialdini's reciprocity principle. Social proof from neighbours validates curiosity as a normal response. "Not here to sell you anything today" disarms resistance before it forms.
Cialdini — reciprocityCialdini — social proofPre-suasion — disarm first
5 — The loss aversion hook
⚡ Most powerful trigger
"Hi [Name], Declan here — quick one. I've got a buyer who missed out on [address] last week and they're still in the market specifically for [suburb]. I don't want to assume, but I'd feel bad not at least asking — is selling something that's ever crossed your mind? Even down the track?"
Why it works: People feel the pain of missing out roughly twice as strongly as gaining something equivalent (Kahneman). "I'd feel bad not asking" shifts the dynamic — it's now them potentially missing out if they say no too quickly.
Loss aversion — KahnemanCialdini — scarcityFOMO trigger
6 — The unity / in-group opener
🤝 Cialdini's 7th principle
"Hi [Name], Declan here. I've been working in [suburb] for a while now and I like to personally call the street after every sale — I'd rather people hear the result from me than read some generic flyer in the letterbox. You're a [suburb] local, so I wanted to make sure you had the actual number."
Why it works: People are far more influenced by those they see as part of their community. Positioning yourself as the local agent activates in-group belonging. "You're a local" makes them feel seen, not targeted.
Cialdini — unityCommunity identityAnti-pitch positioning
7 — The XYZ data opener
Situation → Action → Result
"Hi [Name], Declan here. Quick one — the market in [suburb] has been moving fast: [X homes sold in 60 days, median up X%]. I just sold [address] — ran a full campaign, had [X] buyer groups through, ended up with competing offers. Sold in [X days] for [result]. I'm calling the neighbours because that result affects your value too, and I've got a few buyers still looking. Worth 20 minutes to find out what your place is sitting at?"
Why it works: Specific numbers are nearly impossible to ignore. Vague claims like "the market's hot" lose people — but "sold in 9 days, 4% above asking" creates instant authority and FOMO. Pull your actual data before each session.
XYZ — situation action resultAuthority via specificsData-led credibility

Just listed

Call the 20–30 closest homes before the listing hits the portals. Neighbours often know a buyer — or become one. One of the easiest ways to generate your next appraisal for free.

1 — The exclusive heads up
Start here
"Hi [Name], it's Declan — I'm actually calling before this hits realestate.com.au. I've just listed [address] and I always give the neighbours a heads up first. Sometimes the best buyer for a home in a street is someone who already loves the area."
Why it works: "Before it hits the portals" triggers exclusivity (Cialdini's scarcity) and positions you as doing them a favour. It reframes the call from a pitch to a privilege.

Buyer database

People who registered at your opens 1–5+ years ago. You have no idea if they bought, rented, or are still looking. Diagnose first, pitch second.

Diagnose before you pitch

You have zero context. Their answer to the first question tells you everything. Branch to the right script from there.

The app checks for updates each time you open it, and you can check any time with the button below. On Windows a new version installs itself and asks you to restart.
