Just sold street

You've just sold a property on their street. That result is your reason to call every neighbour. Full call flow from opener to close, plus follow-up for every scenario.

The opener
"Hi [Name], it's Declan here — I'm one of the local agents around [suburb]. We haven't met, so I'll keep this genuine and quick. I just helped some of your neighbours sell their place on [street], and I'm letting a few people nearby know how it went before word gets around. Is now an okay moment for a quick one?"
Then it goes one of two ways
They're happy to talk
1 — Give them the result
"So [address] just sold — [X days on market, $X]. That's well above what most people in the street would have guessed, and it does move the number on your place too."
2 — Make it about them
"How long have you been in the street yourself? … And has selling ever crossed your mind, even down the track?"
3 — The ask
"I'm in [suburb] this week anyway — it'd take me 20 minutes to walk through and give you a real number rather than a guess. Is that something you'd want me to do for you?"
If they're not ready for that
"No worries at all — can I text you the sold data for your street so you've got it? What's the best mobile?"
They push back
Someone else answers
Wrong person / partner picks up
"Oh sorry — I was after [Name]. It's Declan, one of the local agents. Nothing urgent at all: I sold [address] on your street and I've been letting the neighbours know how it went. Are you at that address as well? … Then honestly you'd want to hear it too."
If they'd rather you call back
"No problem — when's usually a better time to catch you both? I'll make a note and try then."
Renting, not the owner
"Ah, you're renting — thanks for letting me know, I'll leave you be. Sorry to bother you."
Nobody picks up

Mirroring: Repeat their last 2–3 words back as a question — "Not interested?" — then stop talking. Gets them explaining instead of hanging up.
Timing: Wed & Thu outperform all other days. Best windows 8–9 AM and 4–5 PM.
Soft assumptive close
"Look, I'm in [suburb] this week anyway — it'd take me 20 minutes to walk through and give you a rough number on what your place would sell for right now. Would Tuesday morning or Wednesday afternoon suit you better?"
Alternative close (if hesitant)
"Even if you're not thinking of selling, having a current figure is useful — for insurance, planning, whatever. No cost, no obligation. Which works better, early or later in the week?"
The text close (low-commitment exit)
"No worries at all — can I send you the recent sold data for your street by text? Just so you've got it. What's the best mobile?"

Just listed

Call the 20–30 closest homes before the listing hits the portals. Neighbours often know a buyer — or become one. One of the easiest ways to generate your next appraisal for free.

1 — The exclusive heads up
Start here
"Hi [Name], it's Declan — I'm actually calling before this hits realestate.com.au. I've just listed [address] and I always give the neighbours a heads up first. Sometimes the best buyer for a home in a street is someone who already loves the area."
Why it works: "Before it hits the portals" triggers exclusivity (Cialdini's scarcity) and positions you as doing them a favour. It reframes the call from a pitch to a privilege.

Best for finding buyers

3 — The double pivot
Best for potential sellers

And on your own end — have you ever thought about upsizing, downsizing, or a change of scenery? If the timing ever lined up, I could show you through before anyone else gets a look."

Why it works: In-person engagement converts far higher than a call. People who walk through may realise they want to sell. "Neighbours only" uses unity and exclusivity together.

Buyer database

People who registered at your opens 1–5+ years ago. You have no idea if they bought, rented, or are still looking. Diagnose first, pitch second.

Diagnose before you pitch

You have zero context. Their answer to the first question tells you everything. Branch to the right script from there.

The diagnostic opener — use every time
Start here
"Hi [Name], it's Declan here — I know this might be a little out of the blue. You came through one of my open homes in [suburb] a while back, and I just wanted to touch base and see how things ended up for you. Did you manage to find something in the area, or is the search still ongoing?"

How updates work
The app checks for updates each time you open it, and you can check any time with the button below. On Windows a new version installs itself and asks you to restart. On Mac it opens the download for you — just open the .dmg and drag the app into your Applications folder, replacing the old one.
